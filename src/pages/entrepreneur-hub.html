Guide 1

How to validate a business idea

Before you spend months building, find out whether people genuinely want what you plan to offer — and whether they'll pay for it.

  • Describe the problem in one sentence. Who has it, and how do they solve it today?
  • Talk to 10–20 potential customers. Ask about their experience, not whether they like your idea.
  • Study the alternatives. Competitors prove demand exists; look for gaps you can fill.
  • Test with a simple offer. A one-page website, a WhatsApp catalogue or a pre-order shows real intent.

Guide 2

First steps for new entrepreneurs

You don't need everything figured out on day one. You need a clear next step, then the one after that.

  • Define your first offer. One product or service, one type of customer, one price.
  • Keep costs low. Start lean and invest as you learn what works.
  • Sort the basics. A business name, a professional email, a simple way to take payments, and the registrations your business type requires.
  • Set a 90-day goal. Something measurable, like your first ten paying customers.

Guide 4

Finding your first customers

Your first customers rarely come from big campaigns. They come from people, conversations and being easy to find.

  • Start with your network. Tell friends, family and past colleagues exactly what you offer and who it's for.
  • Go where your customers are. Local groups, communities, events and online forums.
  • Make a compelling first offer. A launch price or bonus lowers the risk of trying something new.
  • Ask for reviews and referrals. Happy early customers are your best marketing.

Guide 5

Building an online presence on a budget

You can look professional online without a big budget. Focus on the few things that matter most.

  • Claim your Google Business Profile. It's free and helps local customers find and contact you.
  • Pick one or two social channels. Be consistent where your customers already are.
  • Create a simple website or landing page. Clear offer, contact details and an enquiry form.
  • Make it easy to reach you. Clickable phone, email and WhatsApp on every page.

Guide 6

Motivation grounded in realistic lessons

Every successful business owner has faced slow months, failed ideas and moments of doubt. The difference is usually persistence and learning, not luck.

  • Progress beats perfection. Launch, learn and improve.
  • Small, consistent actions compound. One post, one call, one improvement at a time.
  • Feedback is a gift. Every "no" tells you something useful.
  • Ask for help early. Mentors, peers and partners shorten the learning curve.
